---
title: Nothing broke at 06:00 and the damage showed up three steps later
tags: automation, devops, reliability, ai
---

We have a job that is supposed to run daily at a fixed time. Call it 06:00. It does run daily. It has never silently disappeared, never been deleted, never quietly failed to trigger. And still, on many days, it starts tens of minutes after the time we wrote down. Not because our code is slow to start, but because the platform's own scheduler is busy at that hour and hands out start times in the order it can manage them. "At 06:00" and "close to 06:00" turned out to be two different guarantees, and we had bought the first one while paying for the second.

The interesting part is that this cost us nothing for a long time. A job that finishes at 06:40 instead of 06:05 is, considered alone, completely fine. Nobody is watching at 06:05. Nothing is on fire. The output lands, correct and complete, half an hour later than the number in the config file. If that job were the only thing in the system, the drift would be a curiosity worth one line in a wiki.

What made it expensive is that we had written 06:00 into more than one place. Not literally in most cases — we are not that careless — but semantically. Other parts of the operation were built on the belief that by the time they woke up, the 06:00 job had already done its work. That belief is not a config value. It lives in the spacing between scheduled things: you put one job at 06:00 and the next at 07:00 and you have silently declared that an hour is enough. Nobody writes that down as an assumption, because it does not feel like an assumption. It feels like arithmetic.

So the failure, when it came, was not in the job that drifted. It was in a later step that ran on time, found less than it expected, and did the perfectly reasonable thing with what it found. It did not crash. It did not warn. It behaved exactly as designed for the inputs it was handed. The log for the drifted job said it succeeded, because it did. The log for the downstream job said it succeeded, because by its own definition it had. Two green results, one bad outcome, and no single place where a human could point and say that thing is broken.
